2. The causal main chain

1
Property rights (Roman law; feudal tenure) → Magna Carta constraint on the king (1215)

Barons defend their property and feudal rights against arbitrary royal taxation and seizure.

↓
2
Magna Carta → Slow British constitutionalism

Repeated reissues (1216/1217/1225) and confirmations embed the idea that the king is under law; Parliament grows via consent to taxation.

↓
3
Slow British constitutionalism → Glorious Revolution (1688) and Locke (1690)

Civil War and 1688 settle parliamentary supremacy; Locke theorizes property, consent, right of resistance.

↓
4
Glorious Revolution / Locke → Enlightenment

British liberty becomes a model for Voltaire, Montesquieu, Rousseau; builds on Cartesian rationalism.

↓
5
Enlightenment → French Revolution (1789)

Rights and popular sovereignty applied in a rigid absolutist state produce rupture, regicide, Napoleon.

↓
6
French Revolution / British stability → Industrial Revolution and capitalism

Secure property and stable institutions in Britain enable investment in steam power and factories.

↓
7
Industrial capitalism → Marx's reaction (1848)

Factory conditions and inequality prompt communism as a critique of private ownership of capital.

↓
8
Marx's reaction → Cold War and welfare states

1917 revolution leads to a US-USSR rivalry; the West adopts welfare states; USSR collapses 1991.

↓
9
Cold War / welfare states → Today's tensions (Oct 30, 2026)

Unresolved questions about markets, state, and sovereignty show up in market volatility, Russia-EU conflict, rule-of-law fights, populist unrest, polarized US politics (as discussed on the call).

3. The accidents

Contingent events that bent the chain.

🎲 Edward the Confessor's broken succession promise
⟶
Disputed succession leads to the Norman Conquest of 1066 and ties England to Normandy and France.
🎲 John loses Normandy (1204) and fails to retake it (1214)
⟶
Ruinous taxes trigger the baronial revolt, which produces Magna Carta (1215).
🎲 John dies in 1216 leaving a 9-year-old heir
⟶
Regents reissue Magna Carta to win support, which preserves it.
🎲 Black Death (1347-51) labor shortage
⟶
Peasant bargaining power rises and serfdom weakens in England (Peasants' Revolt 1381).
🎲 Henry VIII's lack of a male heir
⟶
Annulment dispute leads to the break with Rome (1534) and the English Reformation.
🎲 Stuart succession and James II's Catholic heir (1688)
⟶
Invitation to William of Orange leads to the Glorious Revolution.
🎲 Cheap coal and flooded mines in Britain
⟶
Demand for pumping engines (Newcomen, Watt) helps launch the Industrial Revolution.

4. Conclusion: history is a K-line

Ryan's view: history is driven by accidents, so it is contingent, irrational, even absurd. Yet over the long run it trends upward, like a stock K-line (candlestick) chart: many folds and pullbacks, with a rising overall direction.

History is accidental, irrational, absurd: a broken promise, a lost duchy, a child king, a plague, a missing male heir. Each candle is noise. Yet zoom out and the chart climbs, from conditional feudal tenure to rights, constitutions, industry, and welfare states. It rises like a stock K-line, with folds and pullbacks along the way.
